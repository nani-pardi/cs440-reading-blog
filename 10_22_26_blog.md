## Key Idea

AI training collectives are bursty, and in a lossy commodity fabric one dropped packet becomes a straggler that holds up the whole collective. dcSim tests the path first: each packet sends a tiny "Sim" probe down a random ECMP path through a separate, rate-limited priority queue that acts like a mini version of the data queue. If the probe makes it, the real packet follows the same path after a fixed delay. On a simulated 128-host fat-tree, it gets zero data loss, 10–12.5% faster mixture-of-experts collectives, and 45% of the alternatives' completion time when the network is oversubscribed.

## Critique

Reservations from just priority queues and a token bucket is neat, and the oversubscription result is convincing. The guarantee is softer than it sounds, though. The paper's own counterexample shows drops can still happen across multiple stages, and staying lossless needs data buffers twice as big as the Sim ones. I'd also want hardware results, a mixed-transport test, and the cost to small flows.

## Connections

[DCTCP](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1851275.1851192) reacts to ECN after queues build up. A probe that survives is a proactive signal that the no signal paper never covered. dcSim also picks paths with random ports, the exact randomness P-ECMP gets rid of.
