## Key Idea

WAN operators usually plan for at most k failures (typically k ≤ 2) under fixed demand. Yet Microsoft's outage in Africa came from several fiber cuts at once plus shifting demand. Raha hunts for the *probable* failures and demand matrix that make the failed network look worst compared to the healthy one (its design point). It does this by treating the problem as heuristic analysis on top of MetaOpt's bilevel optimizer. It works with any single-shot traffic engineering scheme, handles partial capacity loss within link bundles and backup-path failover, and finds degradations at least 2× bigger than two-failure tools do.

## Critique

The big insight is comparing against the design point. If you just search for the worst outcome, you get silly answers like zero demand. Running in production as an alerting tool, plus a capacity-augmentation loop that clears probable degradations in a few steps, makes a strong case. The weaker spots: failure probabilities come from per-link repair history, everything is normalized, and clustering gives up about 15% of the degradation for speed.

## Connections

[B4](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2486001.2486019) runs links hot because central traffic engineering can reroute. Raha asks how much of that slack is left when probable failures pile up. Like
the partial reachability ones, the interesting case is partial loss, not total failure.
